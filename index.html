The problem

If the buyer denies it later, what proof do you have?

01

Your billing software only tracks the bill

It shows what you invoiced. It can't tell you whether the buyer actually received the goods on the terms you both agreed.

02

Trading apps stop once the deal is made

They help you find the buyer. After that, the deal moves to phone and WhatsApp like it always did — no proof either side can point to.

03

A GST invoice proves tax, not acceptance

Your e-invoice is valid for the government. It says nothing about whether the buyer agreed the goods reached them as promised.

What it actually runs on

We didn't reinvent SMS, payments, or cloud storage.

Your link reaches the buyer's phone through MSG91, registered the way Indian transactional SMS is legally required to be. Receipts and every archived record live on AWS — the same infrastructure banks run their systems on. Your subscription is billed through Razorpay. The record itself sits in PostgreSQL, chosen because a legal-evidence record needs boring and reliable, not clever. Everything else — Redis, NestJS, Next.js, Docker — is the plumbing that keeps it fast, and something you should never have to think about.
